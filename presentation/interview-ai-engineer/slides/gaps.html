I have thought hard about failure, backpressure and idempotency, but on in-process primitives, not on managed ones. Python is second — the concepts transfer, and Pydantic is recognisably the same job as the schema validation here, but I have not shipped production Python. MCP is interesting rather than embarrassing: I have not implemented it, but the port boundary I already have is the right shape to expose, and I can argue why a tool loop was the wrong choice for this particular pipeline — which is a more useful conversation than claiming I have built one. What I would ask to be judged on is the reasoning: why each control exists, what it cost before it existed, and which failure mode I picked deliberately. That is what transfers. The cloud surface area is learnable and I would not pretend it is already learned.
