Walk the five in order, naming the cost of each — the ordering is the argument. SHA-256 of the message. Costs nothing, no network, catches the most common case: someone forwarding the identical flyer. Deterministic collapse. Several events sharing title, date and location but differing only in time are one gathering described twice — "arrive at 17:00, party 17:30". No model involved. Exact match. Title, date, time and child already in the database, one indexed query. The LLM judge. Same gathering, different framing. One call per sibling event, so it runs late. Calendar overlap. A semantically matching entry already on the Google calendar within sixty minutes — usually one a parent added by hand. The app binds to that entry instead of creating a second. If they ask why five: each catches something the others structurally cannot. Hashing cannot see paraphrase. Embeddings cannot see "the same gathering described differently". Neither can see an entry a human typed into Google Calendar last week.
